A household buys a home
every three minutes.
Then nobody helps them.

Moving into a home costs a Dutch household €50,000 to €100,000 in finishing and furnishing, spread over roughly twenty-three decisions. We start with new build, where every one of those decisions has a contractual deadline and the handover date is published — then extend the same model to existing homes and renovations, which the platform already supports. Chapter 7 · The model

Joining is cheap. Outcomes are what we charge for.

An annual participation fee carries the base. A success fee, banded by job size rather than taken as a percentage, carries the upside.

A band needs only a rough job size, which a supplier will confirm. A percentage needs an exact invoice, which they are motivated to understate. Take rate lands between 0.8% and 2.4% of household spend — low enough that nobody disputes it.

Chapter 8 · Attribution

We do not police attribution. We buy it.

The household hands us the numbers because it gets something it wants in return.

The quote

Uploaded to be price-checked against our benchmark. A product that already exists.

The invoice

Uploaded because it starts warranty tracking and completes the home file.

The compounding

Every confirmed amount improves the benchmark, which makes the next upload more likely.

Attribution is a by-product of two services the household would want even if we charged nothing. That is why the outcome model is billable here and not at a lead marketplace.

Chapter 10 · The founder

I tried this once before, ten years too early

From 2012 to 2016 I worked inside De Mandemakers Groep, a €1.5bn player in exactly this market. In 2016 I started an experimental platform called Bylder. It was too early. I kept the domain. In 2024 I bought a new-build home myself and lived the problem from the inside.

Since May 2026 we have rebuilt it with everything that experience taught us: assemble the hard side first, refuse to sell ranking, and price the outcome rather than the introduction.
