## The category leader is dismantling its own model

Angi Inc (Nasdaq: ANGI) is the global leader in home-services lead generation and the parent
of **Instapro Group** — Amsterdam-headquartered, founded 2021, operating Werkspot (NL),
MyBuilder (UK), MyHammer (DE/AT), Travaux (FR) and Instapro (IT). One model across six
European markets: the tradesperson pays per lead.

That model is being repriced in public.

| Angi Inc | |
|---|---|
| Q1 2025 revenue | $246M — **down 19%** year on year |
| Total shareholder return, 3 years | **−78%** |
| Network Service Requests after its own policy change | **−67%** |
| Leads | **−81%** |

The decline is not an accident. In January 2025 Angi moved to *homeowner choice*: it stopped
selling a request to whichever pro paid, and let the homeowner pick instead. Lead volume fell
81%. In the same period homeowner NPS rose by more than 30 points, churn fell by over 30%, and
repeat rates turned positive for the first time.

**The category leader has concluded that selling leads destroys the marketplace, and is
absorbing an 81% volume loss to get out of it.** That is the single most useful fact in this
chapter, and it is their own disclosure — not our interpretation.

Bylder starts where Angi is painfully trying to arrive.

## The two axes that matter

Every player sits somewhere on two questions:

**When do you arrive?** Late, when the household has already formulated a request
("plasterer, Amsterdam"). Or early, at purchase — before the question exists.

**What do you sell?** Access to a request. Or the resolved outcome.

```
                    SELLS THE OUTCOME
                            ▲
        Enpal · 1Komma5°    │        ← the open seat
        Aira                │           (Bylder)
        (one category,      │
         €1.5bn+ of capital)│
   ─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────▶
   ARRIVES LATE             │            ARRIVES EARLY
   (request already formed) │      (at purchase, before
                            │       the question exists)
        Angi · Instapro     │        Houzz
        Thumbtack · Bark    │        (early-ish, but
        Checkatrade         │         inspiration only —
        Homedeal            │         no decision structure)
                            ▼
                    SELLS ACCESS TO A REQUEST
```

The upper-right quadrant — arriving early, across categories, selling the outcome — is empty
in Europe. Not because nobody thought of it, but because it requires two things at once: a
structured model of what a home actually needs, and presence at the moment of purchase. The
vertical installers have the outcome but bought it with capital and only in one category. The
marketplaces have the breadth but arrive after the decision is already made.

## What we do differently, in four points

**1. We arrive before the question exists.** Werkspot is found by someone who already knows
they need a plasterer. We are present when someone buys a home and does not yet know what the
next fourteen months will ask of them. Handover dates and completion dates are knowable —
we track 976 new-build projects covering at least 38,957 homes.

**2. We trade in decisions, not leads.** A lead is a name and a phone number. A decision is a
specification with a date and a budget: which room, which spec, closing when, worth roughly
what. Our ontology holds 25 residential spaces, each with the decisions that fall in them, the
phase they fall in, the cost range and the trades involved. Demand reaches the market already
formed.

**3. We do not sell position.** Ranking is not for sale, at any price. This is the same
published constraint that governs our sister platform in electric mobility, and it is why the
model survives both regulation on ranking transparency and the arrival of AI assistants that
route around paid placement. Revenue comes from the transaction, not the listing.

**4. We install nothing.** No crews, no vans, no inventory. Enpal, 1Komma5° and Aira raised
over €1.5bn to own execution in one category. We map execution across all of them and never
carry it on the balance sheet.

## The long-term claim

As households increasingly ask an AI assistant rather than a search box, the party holding a
structured, current model of what a home needs — and what those decisions actually cost and
when they close — becomes the layer the assistant queries. Directories are summarised away.
A decision graph is not: it is the answer.

That is the asset. Everything else in this memo is how we get there.
