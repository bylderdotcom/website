# Foreword — Ten years, one idea, and a cost curve that finally moved

> Draft for the Bylder investor memo. English, first person, aimed at Zacua Ventures.
> Kept deliberately short: a foreword earns the right to the rest, it does not pre-empt it.

I spent four years, from 2012 to 2016, inside **De Mandemakers Groep** —
the Dutch kitchen, bathroom and furniture group that today turns over **€1.5 billion** across
more than two hundred stores. That is where I learned how this money actually moves. Not from a market
report: from the inside of the largest player in it.

What I saw was that the spending is enormous and the buyer is alone. A household walks into a
kitchen showroom having already made six decisions it did not know it was making, and will make
twenty more without anyone holding the sequence. Every party in that chain sees one slice.
Nobody sees the programme.

## The first attempt, in 2016

So in 2016 I tried to build what I would now call a solution marketplace. Two of us put in
**€150,000**. [The pitch video from that year still exists](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1TDl4so8W7bnd8W8-1sg9twLNtrb94eeU/view?usp=sharing) — and it describes, ten years
early, more or less what this memo describes: bring the household the whole set of choices, and
connect it to the people who can deliver them.

It did not work, and the reasons were not subtle. Building it meant people — editors,
advisors, coordinators — and that made it capital intensive from day one. We stayed too small
to grow organically, and we did not raise. The company stopped.

I kept the domain. For ten years.

## What changed, and how I found out

In mid-2024 I bought a new-build home. It was handed over at the end of 2025, and I went
through the entire programme myself: the extra-work deadlines, the choices that close before
you know they exist, the money that goes out in eighteen months.

While doing it, I used Bylder for something small. I collected discount vouchers at furniture
and interior stores — for myself and for my neighbours in the same project — and I asked each
store a fixed fee to take part. Stores paid. I did not keep proper books on it, so I will not
put a number on it here.

That is a modest anecdote and I am not going to inflate it. But look at what it was: **a group
of households in one project, all buying at the same time, and suppliers paying to reach
them.** That is the entire thesis of this memo, executed by one person with a spreadsheet, and
it worked.

It gave me the motivation back. It did not give me a plan.

## May 2026

The plan arrived when I built the platform, in May 2026, with AI doing the work that used to
require the team I could not afford in 2016.

The realisation was not that AI is useful. It was narrower and more consequential:
**what I wanted to build ten years ago is now buildable at a fraction of the cost — and the
same shift makes the incumbents attackable for the first time.** In 2016 the legacy
marketplaces were unassailable: they had the traffic, the sales forces and the balance sheets,
and the only route in was to outspend them. That route is gone, and so is their protection.
The category leader is currently dismantling its own lead-generation model in public
(Chapter 2).

The idea did not change. The cost of building it did, and so did the position of the people
already there.

## What I have been doing since

Three things, in this order, since May 2026:

**Distribution** — being findable at the moment the household starts, in search and
increasingly in AI answers.

**The funnel** — one path that has to convince two very different audiences to create an
account: the household, and the business that wants to reach it.

**The tools** — the AI layer that helps someone who has just bought a home make the right
choices about what to buy and who to hire.

Three months in, the supply side is mapped and the demand side is nearly empty. Chapter 3 sets
that out in full, including a decision in July to switch off a quarter of our own pages because
the measurement said they were not working.

I would rather you read that from me than find it.
